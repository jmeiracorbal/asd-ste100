# ASD-STE100 terminology guidance

Use this guidance to decide how to handle technical terminology without assuming a specific subject field.

## General principle

Preserve domain-specific terms when changing them could alter the technical meaning. Do not replace established technical terms only to make them fit general vocabulary.

## Sources of technical terminology

A technical term can come from:

- the subject field;
- an organization;
- a project;
- a product or system;
- an applicable standard or specification;
- controlled source material supplied by the user.

Treat these sources as context. Do not infer terminology from an unrelated domain.

## What to preserve

Preserve a term when it is required to identify a specific concept, item, state, action, measurement, or relationship.

Also preserve, when applicable:

- identifiers;
- symbols;
- units;
- codes;
- names;
- labels;
- part or item designations;
- mandatory nomenclature.

Do not alter capitalization, spelling, punctuation, or formatting when those elements are part of the identifier or approved term.

## One term for one concept

Use one preferred term for one concept throughout the text.

Do not alternate between synonyms only for style.

If the source uses multiple terms for the same concept, select the established or approved term when the context makes that choice clear.

If the context does not make the correct term clear, preserve the source terminology rather than inventing a normalization that can change meaning.

## General vocabulary and technical terminology

Do not treat every uncommon word as an error. A term can be valid because it is necessary technical terminology.

When strict ASD-STE100 lexical compliance is required:

1. Check general vocabulary against the official controlled dictionary.
2. Check non-general terms against the applicable technical terminology rules.
3. Do not invent dictionary approval for a word that has not been verified.

## Terminology changes

Change a technical term only when:

- the replacement is known to mean the same concept;
- the replacement follows the applicable approved terminology;
- the change does not alter an identifier, requirement, measurement, classification, or technical relationship.

Do not simplify terminology at the cost of precision.

## Domain neutrality

Do not assume the content belongs to a particular industry, profession, technology, or subject field.

Use the terminology present in the source material and approved context. Apply the same terminology method regardless of the domain.
